M83 on Your A1 Mini

Two lines keep showing up together in the A1-family start routines the community shares: G90, then M83 on the very next line. I think that pair is worth a close look, because it looks redundant and it isn't.

G90 sets XYZ positioning to absolute. M83 then sets the extruder to relative, and it has to, because G90 refuses to touch the extruder at all.

The extruder is the part of the printer that pushes filament, and every extrusion move carries an E value. A command like M83 is “modal,” which is firmware-speak for staying in force until something changes it, the same way your feed rate F carries from one move to the next. There are two modes. M82 is absolute: each E is the running total since the counter started. M83 is relative: each E is just the plastic for that move. The Prusa firmware reference states the trap plainly for both G90 and G91: “E axis is left intact.” Positioning and extrusion are two independent modal states, each with its own switch.

That is why the start routines the community shares pair the two lines: G90 for where the nozzle goes, M83 for how much plastic it pushes along the way. I haven't seen Bambu's verbatim stock file, but three independent transcriptions of the A1 family's start routine on the Bambu forum all put M83 right after G90, and the numbers corroborate it. Two consecutive moves each carry E.3742, which is 0.3742 mm of filament per 5 mm move. A retraction shows up as a negative E, G1 E-0.5, and it reads instantly in relative mode: pull half a millimeter back. The purge idiom, G92 E0 followed by G1 E50, zeroes the counter and pushes 50 mm. In relative mode those numbers just work. In absolute mode they would be gibberish.

Here is why this is the one parsing habit that matters most for your analysis work. If you read a relative file's E values as absolute totals, “0.37 on this move” becomes “extrude to total 0.37,” and your numbers come out wildly wrong. I treat it the way I treat units: never trust a bare number until you know what it is measured in. For E values, the mode line is the unit. So the next time you open a file, find the M82 or M83 line before you believe any E number.
